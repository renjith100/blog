A lot changes in AI in one week. New models, new features in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, agents that can suddenly book flights or use your Mac. Keeping up usually means following twenty accounts on X and reading a lot of hype to find the few things that actually shipped.

So I keep a page for it. Every week I collect the launches that mattered, and I write each one up in a few plain sentences. It used to be called "Weekly" in the menu, which didn't tell you much. Now it's called **[AI This Week](/weekly)**.

Here's a 40-second tour of how it works: pick a week, scan the list and open an update.

## What you get every week

- **The whole week in one sentence.** If that's all you read, you still know the big picture.
- **Every update that mattered, newest first.** Usually 10 to 15 of them. Each title says what happened, so the list alone is a quick catch-up.
- **A short summary in plain words.** What shipped, who can use it, what it costs, and anything to watch out for. No marketing language.
- **The original source.** Most updates include the company's announcement post or video, plus a link to the blog post or changelog, so you can check it yourself.

## What it covers

The tools people actually use: OpenAI and ChatGPT, Anthropic and Claude, Google and Gemini, xAI and Grok, Meta, Microsoft, Perplexity, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and Apple. The focus is on **AI agents**, the assistants that do work for you instead of only answering questions, because that's where the most is changing right now.

It isn't only good news. When a launch gets a cold reception, or a company like United blocks AI agents from booking flights, that goes in too.

## How it works when you click

1. **Open [AI This Week](/weekly).** It starts on the latest week that has updates, so you never land on an empty page.
2. **Pick a week from the calendar.** Each tile is one week, Monday to Sunday. The number is how many updates it has and the small logos show who shipped them. The dot marks the current week.
3. **Click an update.** It opens in a popup with the summary, the original post or video, and the source link. Close it and you're back at the list.
4. **Share a week or an update.** Every week has its own link, and so does every update, so you can send someone exactly the thing you're talking about.

## Why come back every week

- **Five minutes instead of an hour.** I do the reading and filtering, so you get the result.
- **You stay current without the noise.** You'll know when a new model is out or a feature you've been waiting for arrives, without scrolling through hype.
- **You find things you can use today.** Many updates are features you already have access to and didn't know about.
- **Nothing gets lost.** Every past week stays on the calendar. If you missed a week, or want to see how fast things are moving, you can look back.

Updates are added during the week as things ship, so by Monday the previous week is complete. Monday morning is a good time to check.
