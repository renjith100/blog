I watched a video this week that I found very useful. **[Ben AI](https://www.youtube.com/@BenAI92)** went through Anthropic's research on how people use Claude, about 400,000 Claude Code sessions plus Anthropic's own staff. The people who got the best results weren't the most technical. They were the clearest communicators. Managers scored highest of any group, even on coding tasks.

Full credit goes to Ben for the video: [Anthropic's Study Just Revealed How The Top 1% Uses AI Differently](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=81Jg17dQz1A). I recommend watching it.

## The short version

If you only take one thing away, it's this: **brief Claude the way a good manager briefs a new hire.** Every prompt should cover four parts:

1. **The job.** Describe the whole task, not a list of steps. Decide what gets done and let Claude work out how.
2. **The why.** Give the reason. On a long task Claude makes dozens of small decisions, and knowing why lets it make them the way you would.
3. **Guardrails.** Only the ones that really matter, written plainly with a reason. No capitals and no "CRITICAL".
4. **What done looks like.** One to three checks Claude can verify, and tell it not to stop until they all pass.

The six lessons from the video:

- **Communicate clearly.** Being precise about what you want, not your job title, is what made someone an expert.
- **Get good at feedback.** Experts don't get it right first time more often. They recover more often. When an output is wrong, rewind, fix the original prompt and rerun it instead of piling on corrections.
- **Make output fast to review.** As Claude does more of the work, you become the bottleneck. Ask for replies that lead with what needs your input.
- **Automate what you never get to.** Look for the jobs you never did because they didn't feel worth your time.
- **Give Claude your decisions.** You do the deciding and Claude does the doing, but Claude only knows the decisions it can read.
- **Keep your setup lean.** Old rules, skills and connectors load into every chat. Cut them back when a new model comes out.
