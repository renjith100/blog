Who is asking?

A tired seeker asks Ramana Maharshi, "What is the nature of consciousness?" Ramana doesn't answer. He asks a question back. Everything else here unfolds from that one question.

Notes on a video by One Timeless Witness · watch on YouTube · the player stops at 8:04, before the movie-screen part · any timestamp plays from there

The search itself is the confusion

We look for consciousness as if it were an object hidden somewhere in the body or the mind, or a state of peace or light that will one day come down on us after enough effort. The seeker had spent years on meditation, study and ritual for exactly that. So Ramana starts the other way round: with what consciousness is not. 1:04

The saffron dot is the witness. Watch it stay where it is while everything else changes.

Then ask: who am I?

"You do not stop searching by effort. You see through the search." Not as a mantra to repeat, and not as a question for the mind to answer, but as a living inquiry that turns attention back to its source. Step through the conversation and watch every answer that can be named fall away. 5:22

Who knows it?

The body, the mind and the emotions all say "I". Tap each one. 6:50

The one who knows

You were always the ocean

"Then how do I rest in it?" "You already are. You have never left it. The question is not how to reach it, but how to stop pretending you are something else." 7:16

"You do not break free. You see that you were never bound. Bondage is created by misidentification." 7:43

Sit with it

For when I come back to this page. Pick a length, press Begin, and let one question at a time lead attention back to the one who is asking. Nothing to achieve.
